Stop Seeking Approval From Everyone

The goal is not to stop caring what people think. It is to stop making their reaction the final authority on your choices.

Approval feels safe because it gives immediate social feedback. But when you depend on it, ordinary decisions become negotiations: what should I wear, say, post, choose, or refuse so nobody is disappointed?

Approval becomes expensive

Constantly checking other people's reactions fragments your attention. You start editing yourself before anyone has even asked. Over time, that weakens your ability to hear your own preferences and values.

“You can listen to feedback without surrendering ownership of the decision.”

Use an internal standard

Before asking whether others will approve, ask: Is this honest? Is it useful? Does it match my values? Will I respect this decision tomorrow? These questions do not eliminate uncertainty. They give uncertainty a place inside a larger framework.

Practice with small choices

  • Choose a meal without polling the group.
  • Say no once without inventing a complicated excuse.
  • Publish a useful idea without checking every reaction.
  • Make one decision each day you are willing to own completely.

Confidence grows through evidence. Every time you make a reasonable decision and remain okay with the outcome, you teach your nervous system that you can trust yourself.
